Move playbook

The thinking moves behind each issue's best idea.

Invention move 01 · from No. 001
Arrange, don't add.

The same ingredients in a different order can give opposite results. Before adding something new, ask what rearranging what you have would do.

Example: Charge-patterned supramolecular threads: all-positive killed neurons; a positive anchor between negative arms made them thrive.

Invention move 02 · from No. 001
Let the surroundings flip the switch.

When a design needs opposite properties in different places, tie the property to something that already differs between those places (acidity, temperature, enzymes, salt).

Example: A lipid that is positive at pH 4 to 6 (loading, endosome escape) and negative at blood pH 7.4.

Invention move 03 · from No. 002
Build an adapter, not a blend.

When two parts won't join, look for an existing shape that already speaks both 'languages', and use one small translator piece instead of smearing one design into the other.

Example: A sheet-lattice (TPMS) unit cell whose edges end in strut-like nodes joins stiff sheet lattices to soft strut lattices; seams never failed first.

Invention move 04 · from No. 002
Make the problem fuel the fix.

Find something the target produces in excess and use it as the trigger or raw material for your response, so the fix appears only where, and as much as, the problem exists.

Example: Tumor lactate is turned into peroxide by an enzyme, which crosslinks an alginate gel only within ~1 mm of the cells.
